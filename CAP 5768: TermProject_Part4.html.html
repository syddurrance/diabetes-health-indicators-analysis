Cluster Centroid Analysis¶

Looking at the centroids, the eight clusters basically split the population by age group, and each one tells a pretty clear story about health. Cluster 3 groups the youngest respondents (18-34 plus 40-44) and is the healthiest of the bunch, with the lowest rates of high blood pressure (-0.55), high cholesterol (-0.49), and heart disease (-0.29), along with the highest physical activity. Clusters 0, 2, 4, 5, and 6 move through the 35-39, 45-49, 50-54, 55-59, and 60-64 age bands, and you can watch the health measures get steadily worse at each step. Clusters 7 (70-74) and 1 (65-69, 75-79, and 80+) are the oldest and have the worst chronic condition numbers, with high blood pressure at +0.36 and +0.34 and cholesterol at +0.28 and +0.23. The one measure that does not follow this pattern is BMI, which peaks in middle age (cluster 2 at +0.12) and then drops below average in the two oldest clusters, so the sickest groups are also the lightest. Sex stays within about +/-0.05 across all eight clusters, meaning it did not help separate anyone. Overall, the clusters describe a population that gets progressively less active and more likely to carry a chronic condition as it ages, with weight peaking in the middle years rather than at the end.
